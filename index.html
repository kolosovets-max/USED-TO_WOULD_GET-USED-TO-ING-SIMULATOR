used to / would / be used to

Past habits and familiar routines

Four practice modes on used to, would, be used to and get used to: pick the form, spot the mistake, complete a connected story, and separate a finished habit from something that simply feels normal. Every answer comes with a short note on why a native speaker chooses that form.

Cheat sheet: how a native speaker decides

used to

  • Works with every verb — actions and states alike: be, live, have, know, love.
  • Needs no time marker: I used to smoke makes sense on its own.
  • Highlights the change: that was then, this is now.
  • Usually opens the story and sets the time frame.

would

  • Dynamic actions only — things you could film happening.
  • Needs a time frame: when I was a kid, every summer. Without one it reads as a conditional.
  • Paints a picture and adds nostalgia.
  • Appears inside the story, once the frame is already set.

be / get used to

  • Always followed by -ing or a noun: used to driving, never used to drive.
  • be used to = it feels normal now: I'm used to getting up at six.
  • get used to = the process of growing comfortable: she soon got used to it.
  • Works in any tense: was used to, will get used to, have got used to.
